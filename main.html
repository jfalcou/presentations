--- ## What went wrong ? ### The One Mistake * Tool that do everything are nice on papers. * Integration and implementation are in fact complex. * Learning curve for new contributors is more than steep. ### A New Approach (2018+) * Software development is a complex process. * Libraries are softwares.
**What about applying some of the 30+ years old development strategies?**
--- ## Building Solid Foundations ### The SOLID Principles * **Single Responsibility**: *A class should have one and only one job.* * **Open/Close**: *Software entities should be open for extension but closed for modification.* * **Liskov's Substitution Principle**: *Derived types should complement the base object behaviour.* * **Interface Segregation**: *Many specific client interface are better than one general purpose interface.* * **Dependency Inversion**: *Entities must depend on abstractions, not on concretions.* ### Why SOLID? * More a set of guidelines than a cook book. * Prevent **design error** early on. * Helps compare design choices.
**Does those OO rules make sense to design generic libraries?**

* The most general SOLID rules are directly usable. * Incorporate non-member functions into types's interfaces. * Shift focus from Interface/Class/Object to Concept/Type/Value. * Emphasis entities collaboration across libraries. ### Our SOLID Library Principles * **Single Responsibility**: *A **type** or **function** should have one and only one job.* * **Open/Close**: *Software entities extension is a **core design choice**.* * **Substitution Principle**: *Derived types should complement the base object behaviour.* * **Interface Segregation**: *Many specific purpose **libraries** are better than a general purpose **library**.* * **Dependency Inversion**: *Entities must depend on **concepts**, not on **types**.* --- # Parting Words
